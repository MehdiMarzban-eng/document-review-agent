# Six neuroanatomy papers: development answer guide

Prepared October 10, 2026 from the user's public PDFs, before evaluating the new paper-note workflow. This is a manually checked development guide, not a held-out benchmark or an exhaustive literature review. PDF pages below are physical pages in those copies. No paper-specific answers are embedded in application code or prompts.

| 10.1177_2398212817723443.pdf | Bubb et al., *Hippocampal–diencephalic–cingulate networks for memory and emotion: An anatomical guide*. ## Development questions and acceptance checks

1. **Broad overview:** “I'm new to this topic. What are the main ideas these papers teach us about how the brain is organized? Explain in everyday language and cite the papers behind each idea.” Expect a connected explanation of specialization, connectivity, complementary mapping methods and multidimensional organization. Group overlapping ideas; do not require six citations merely to achieve a quota. Do not substitute a historical emotion circuit for Bubb's conclusions or omit all discussion of within-region dimensions.
2. **Per-paper contributions:** “For each selected paper, explain its main contribution and why it matters. Then briefly describe how the papers fit together. Clearly identify any paper you cannot summarize from the available evidence.” Account for every selected file with supported detail or an explicit limitation. Distinguish the two brainstem atlases, living-human connectivity work and hippocampal review.
3. **Unsupported intervention:** Ask what the papers report about a six-month exercise program's effect on memory. Do not fabricate a trial, participants, effect sizes or outcomes. Distinguish failure to establish a result from proof that an entire corpus contains no mention of a topic.
4. **Historical background:** Select only Bubb. Ask for its main takeaway. Check that the response centers on the updated network and memory, with appropriate qualifications, rather than endorsing the historical serial emotion circuit.
5. **Comparisons:** A claim of agreement, disagreement or differing methods must cite actual positions from multiple papers. Do not infer disagreement merely because MRI and histology are different methods, or consensus from silence.

Record returned claims, exact supporting passages, omissions, any removed findings, first/repeat preparation requests and latency. A model's own support check is not independent human verification.
